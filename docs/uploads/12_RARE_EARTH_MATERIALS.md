## Why Rare Earths Are Hit

The Hormuz crisis impacts rare earth supply chains through three indirect but powerful mechanisms:

```
Hormuz closure
    │
    ├─→ Sulfur supply disruption (24% of global seaborne trade)
    │       └─→ Sulfuric acid feedstock shortage
    │               ├─→ Nickel/cobalt refining halted (HPAL process)
    │               │       └─→ EV battery production constrained
    │               └─→ Phosphate fertilizer production reduced
    │
    ├─→ Geopolitical escalation → Iran isolated
    │       └─→ Iran's rare earth deposits (17M+ tonnes) fully inaccessible
    │               └─→ Long-term supply diversification efforts disrupted
    │
    ├─→ Energy shock → REE processing cost inflation
    │       └─→ China dominance leverage increases (90% of global refining)
    │               └─→ Western REE supply chains under strain
    │
    └─→ Shipping route diversions
            └─→ Chinese REE exports to Japan/SK/EU via longer routes (Suez/Cape)
                    └─→ Transit delays; pricing uncertainty
```

## Critical Insight: Sulfur (Often Overlooked)

**Sulfur is the hidden linchpin:**

- **24% of global seaborne sulfur trade** passes through Hormuz
- ~50% of seaborne sulfur originates from Middle East crude oil refining
- Sulfur is feedstock for **sulfuric acid**, which is used in:
  - **HPAL (high-pressure acid leaching):** Refining nickel/cobalt for EV batteries
  - **Phosphate fertilizer production:** High-analysis fertilizers essential for soil fertility
  - Industrial chemicals, metal refining

**Indonesia impact:** Indonesia produces 50%+ of global nickel; imports ~75% of its sulfur from the Middle East. Sulfur shortage is forcing industrial slowdowns across nickel/battery supply chains.

**Result:** The sulfur shortage may be **more immediately disruptive to EV production** than rare earth element shortages, because it blocks nickel/cobalt refining.

## Iran's Rare Earth Reserves: A Stranded Asset

Iran possesses **one of the world's largest rare earth reserves** — estimates range from 8.5 million to 17+ million tonnes (rankings vary by source; Iran likely ranks #2–#3 globally after China and Vietnam).

**Key deposits:**
- Esfordi phosphate deposit (Yazd Province) — REE-bearing
- Chadormalu iron-apatite deposit — REE anomalies identified
- Central Iran exploration zone — 7,000 sq km with REE anomalies

**Pre-crisis development:**
- Iran opened its first **monazite processing plant** (Abbas Abad Industrial Zone) in April 2025
- Iran announced massive lithium discoveries (Hamadan, 2023)
- Iran was positioning itself as a non-Western rare earth supplier

**Since Feb 28, 2026:**
- All REE extraction and processing operations **completely halted** under wartime conditions
- Iran's nascent monazite industry is inaccessible
- Long-term REE supply diversification away from China is now constrained
- This removes a potential hedge against Chinese REE leverage

## China's 90% Refining Monopoly

**The dominance:**
- China controls **90–95% of global rare earth element refining** (separation and purification)
- China controls 60–65% of global rare earth mining (but mining is less critical than refining)
- This dominance is downstream **processing**, not extraction

**2025–2026 export controls:**
- China introduced selective export restrictions on dysprosium, terbium, and other heavy rare earths
- External markets experienced **price spikes up to 6x** relative to Chinese domestic prices
- European export license approvals fell below 25% in some sectors
- China is not weaponizing scarcity—it is **weaponizing control** through licensing cycles

**Price divergence (April 2026):**
- **Dysprosium:** $931/kg (Western) vs. $200/kg (China) = **366% premium**
- **Terbium:** $4,029/kg (Western) vs. $1,150/kg (China) = **348% premium**

## Critical Rare Earth Materials & Their Uses

### Light Rare Earths (LRE) — Abundant, Less Restricted

| Element | Primary Use | 2026 Status |
|---------|---|---|
| **Lanthanum (La)** | FCC (fluid catalytic cracking) catalysts in oil refineries; optical glass; batteries | Refinery disruptions reduce FCC demand; moderate exposure |
| **Cerium (Ce)** | Catalytic converters; catalysts; polishing compounds; ceria-yttria coatings | Automotive & industrial demand reduced |

### Heavy Rare Earths (HRE) — Scarce, China-Controlled, HIGH RISK

| Element | Primary Use | 2026 Impact | Price Shock |
|---------|---|---|---|
| **Neodymium (Nd)** | **NdFeB permanent magnets for EV motors, wind turbines, hard drives** | Critical shortage risk | NdPr alloy: $53→$126/kg (138% YTD gain) |
| **Praseodymium (Pr)** | NdFeB magnets; aerospace alloys | EV production bottleneck | Surged with NdPr complex |
| **Dysprosium (Dy)** | High-temp NdFeB magnets; reactor control rods; defense systems | Extreme scarcity; Chinese export controls active | **+105% to $931/kg** |
| **Terbium (Tb)** | Fuel cells; high-performance magnets; defense electronics | Military procurement threatened | **+103% to $4,029/kg** |
| **Europium (Eu)** | LED phosphors; display technology; medical imaging | Consumer electronics supply chain stress | Elevated 2–3x baseline |
| **Yttrium (Y)** | Superconductors; fuel cells; high-strength alloys | Advanced manufacturing constrained | Premium widening |

## Most Exposed Sectors & Companies

### Electric Vehicles — CRITICAL EXPOSURE

- **100 kWh EV traction motor requires:** ~5 kg neodymium-praseodymium + ~1 kg dysprosium oxide
- **Global EV sales 2026:** 22.9M units forecast (↑28% YoY)
- **Supply crunch timing:** NdPr at all-time highs in April 2026; dysprosium 105% spike YTD
- **Result:** EV manufacturers face choice: absorb massive COGS increases, delay production, or source from Chinese suppliers at premium pricing

**Most exposed OEMs:** Tesla (global supply chain), VW Group, BYD, Li Auto, Geely-Volvo, NIO

### Defense & Aerospace — CRITICAL EXPOSURE

- **F-35 fighter jet:** 418 kg rare earths per unit
- **Type 51 naval destroyer:** 2,600 kg per unit
- **Virginia-class submarine:** 4,600 kg per unit
- **Constraint:** Military contractors/aerospace require superior temperature resistance, magnetic stability — **cannot substitute**

**Exposure:** US DoD, UK MOD, NATO allied defense contractors, Lockheed Martin, Raytheon, BAE Systems

### Renewable Energy — HIGH EXPOSURE

- **Wind turbine permanent magnet generators:** 600–1,000 kg rare earths per MW capacity
- **Shortage timing:** Critical as world accelerates grid decarbonization
- **Result:** Wind farm construction delays; cost escalation

**Companies:** Vestas, Siemens Gamesa, GE Renewable Energy, Ørsted

### Battery & Nickel Refining — INDIRECT but SEVERE

- Sulfur shortage → nickel/cobalt refining halted in Indonesia and Africa
- This may be **more immediately disruptive** to EV battery supply than rare earth shortage
- **Companies:** Glencore, Vale, Tsingshan (nickel production); CATL, LG Energy (battery mfg)

## Downstream Cascade: The Race for Non-Chinese Supply

The Hormuz crisis has accelerated a geopolitical race to break China's REE refining monopoly:

**US Strategic Response:**
- Defense Production Act activations for domestic REE refining
- Texas Rare Earth Holdings, MP Materials (Mountain Pass, CA) ramping production
- Timeline: 12–36 months to meaningfully reduce China dependence (Phase I)

**EU Strategic Response:**
- Critical Minerals Act (2023) targets 25% non-China refining by 2030
- Lynas Rare Earths (Australia) expanding EU processing capacity
- Timeline: 2027–2028 for Phase I capacity

**India & Japan Joint Ventures:**
- Japan pursuing Vietnam/Myanmar rare earth sourcing
- India exploring its own REE deposits
- Timeline: 2028+ for material impact

**Australia Positioning:**
- Lynas Rare Earths operates the world's largest REE refinery outside China (Kula Lumpur, Malaysia)
- Expanding capacity amid crisis
- Strategic supplier for US/EU/Japan alliance

**Risk:** All alternative refining capacity will take 2–3 years to scale. **Until 2028–2029, China's 90% monopoly remains functionally unchallenged.**

## Turkey Angle

Turkey has **minimal REE exposure** directly, but is affected indirectly:

- **Turkish automotive & industrial companies:** Rely on NdFeB magnets, premium-priced from China (Bosch, Arçelik, Vestel supply chains)
- **Turkish defense industrial base:** Limited rare earth dependence; mostly imported finished components
- **Opportunity:** Turkey's **rare earth exploration potential** is low; Turkey is not positioned as alternative source
- **Strategic alignment:** Turkey may benefit from participating in Western REE supply chain diversification initiatives (2027+)

**Turkish companies with indirect exposure:** Aselsan (defense electronics), Arçelik (appliances/motors), Vestel (electronics)
