## 1. Time Frame Conventions (Used Throughout)

These are explicit, so probability statements later are unambiguous:

| Label | Duration | What It Covers |
|-------|----------|----------------|

| **ST — Short Term** | 2–8 weeks | Spring top-dressing fertilizer application window (critical now!) |
| **MT — Mid Term** | 2–6 months | Through 2026 summer harvest; CBRT reaction; budget revision |
| **LT — Long Term** | 6–24 months | 2026/27 planting cycle; structural capacity investments |

**The calendar is the single most important variable.** Turkey is **right now** (mid-April 2026) in the **critical spring fertilizer top-dressing window** for winter wheat and in the **pre-plant window** for cotton (April–May) and corn (April–June). Missing this window permanently damages the 2026 harvest. There is no "catch up later." This is why the next 2–8 weeks matter more than the next 6 months.

### 2.2 The Compound Shock — Three Overlapping Crises

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  2025 Drought (yield already depressed)                │
│      +                                                 │
│  2026 Fertilizer supply shock (+77% urea)              │
│      +                                                 │
│  TRY at record low (44.5/USD) — amplifies import costs │
│      =                                                 │
│  2026–2027 Food inflation + rural income shock         │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Each component alone is manageable. Together they create a non-linear shock because:
1. Farmers enter 2026 with **depleted cash reserves** (2025 drought lowered income).
2. They face **higher input prices in USD** (fertilizer up 40–77%).
3. Those USD costs are multiplied by **record-weak TRY**.
4. Credit access is constrained because **CBRT paused easing**.

**My reasoning stress-test:** Could I be overstating this? Yes — if (a) the government floods the market with subsidized fertilizer from TMO/Tarım Kredi Kooperatifi reserves, and (b) Russian/Iranian fertilizer quietly continues via grey channels. Evidence for (a) is partial (tariff cut is real, but scale of reserves unclear). Evidence for (b) is anecdotal. I keep the core thesis but flag that the **downside scenarios below have ~15% lower probability than raw data suggests** because Turkey has a history of state intervention cushioning rural shocks.

## 3. Turkey's Planting Calendar — Why Timing Is Everything

| Crop | Planting | Key Fertilizer Windows | Harvest | % of Ag Value |
|------|----------|------------------------|---------|---------------|
| **Winter wheat** | Oct–Nov 2025 | Top-dressing: **Feb–Apr 2026** ← NOW | Jun–Jul 2026 | ~15–20% |
| **Barley** | Oct–Nov 2025 | Top-dressing: **Feb–Apr 2026** ← NOW | May–Jul 2026 | ~3–5% |
| **Cotton** (Çukurova/Aegean) | Apr–May 2026 | Pre-plant + top-dress: **Apr–Aug 2026** ← NOW | Sep–Nov 2026 | ~5–8% |
| **Corn** (Central Anatolia) | Apr–Jun 2026 | Pre-plant + side-dress: **Apr–Jul 2026** ← NOW | Sep–Nov 2026 | ~5–7% |
| **Sugar beet** | Mar–Apr 2026 | Full season Apr–Sep | Sep–Nov 2026 | ~2–3% |
| **Hazelnuts** | Perennial | Mar–Apr (key) | Aug–Sep 2026 | ~$3B exports |

**The punchline:** April–May 2026 is when ~60% of Turkey's annual fertilizer is applied. A 4–8 week supply gap right now is effectively permanent yield damage for the 2026 harvest.

## 5. Two Scenarios

### Scenario A — De-escalation ("Crisis gets better")

**Narrative:** Diplomatic pressure (France/UK-led naval coalition, Qatar/Pakistan mediation) forces a renewed ceasefire within 4–8 weeks. Hormuz partially reopens for commercial shipping with insurance pooling. Gulf fertilizer producers restart shipments. Urea spot prices retrace from >$600/MT toward $400/MT by late summer. Turkey's spring planting salvaged via ~60–70% normal fertilizer application (subsidized imports + Russian pipeline + Algerian alternatives).

**Probability of Scenario A:** **~20–30%** as of 2026-04-23 (↓ from 30–40% on Apr 14). Apr 18 Iran re-closure and Apr 22 ship seizures further reduce de-escalation probability. Pakistan talks failed; no credible diplomatic track visible.

**Trigger events to watch for (confirming Scenario A):**
- [A1] Successful UK/France multilateral naval escort mission begins (probability: 35–45%)
- [A2] Iran-US return to talks with Turkey/Qatar as mediators (probability: 30–40%)
- [A3] Urea spot price drops below $500/MT within 4 weeks (probability: 25–35%)
- [A4] QatarEnergy lifts force majeure on fertilizer/LNG (probability: 20–30%)
- [A5] BIST-100 breaks above 15,000 on sustained volume (sentiment confirmation)

**Economic impact under Scenario A:**
- 2026 yield damage limited to 5–10% below trend
- Food inflation adds 1.5–3.0 pp to headline CPI
- Agri exports: $26–29B (vs. $31B target)
- TRY stabilises at 44–46/USD range
- **Scenario A = painful but absorbable**

---

### Scenario B — Continued / Escalating Crisis ("Crisis continues and gradually escalates")

**Narrative:** US blockade continues; no ceasefire through Q2 2026. Iran escalates with asymmetric attacks on non-US Gulf infrastructure. Hormuz remains closed for 3–6+ months. Kirkuk–Ceyhan pipeline contract lapses in July 2026 without renewal; Iraqi oil flow disrupted. Turkey faces fertilizer rationing for spring top-dressing. Government imposes bread price controls; informal market for fertilizer emerges. CBRT hikes 500–750 bps in MT window. TRY breaks 50/USD by July.

**Probability of Scenario B:** **~55–65%** as of 2026-04-23 (↑ from 45–55% on Apr 14). **[B1] TRIGGERED**: Urea spot price at $850/tonne, well above the $700 threshold. Dual blockade now in effect; Iran re-closed Apr 18, seized 2 ships Apr 22. This is currently the confirmed base case.

**Trigger events to watch for (confirming Scenario B):**
- [B1] Urea spot price breaches $700/MT (probability: 40–55%)
- [B2] Turkish food CPI month-over-month print exceeds 5% in May or June (probability: 45–60%)
- [B3] CBRT emergency rate hike announcement (probability: 35–50%)
- [B4] Kirkuk–Ceyhan contract NOT renewed by July 2026 (probability: 30–45%)
- [B5] Turkey declares food emergency / activates emergency grain imports via TMO (probability: 35–50%)
- [B6] Iran attacks on UAE/Saudi LNG/fertilizer infrastructure deeper than Feb–Mar (probability: 25–40%)
- [B7] TRY breaks 50/USD (probability: 50–65%)

**Economic impact under Scenario B:**
- 2026 yield damage 15–25% below trend (compounded with 2025 drought: effectively a lost year)
- Food inflation adds 4.0–8.0 pp to headline CPI (headline CPI back above 50%)
- Agri exports: $20–24B (sharp miss vs. $31B target)
- Potential social unrest in rural/peri-urban areas by Q3 2026
- **Scenario B = severe, potentially destabilizing**

## 9. Challenges to My Own Reasoning (Devil's Advocate)

I should be explicit about where my analysis could be wrong:

1. **"Turkey is #2 vulnerable" framing may overstate urgency.** Brazil's vulnerability is driven by soybean/corn scale and export dependency, which is different in nature. Turkey's vulnerability is more manageable with targeted state intervention. I weight this ~20% less severe than raw data suggests.

2. **State intervention capacity is real.** TMO and Tarım Kredi Kooperatifi can flood the domestic market with subsidized fertilizer faster than most countries. If they tap strategic reserves (currently unknown size — see [[OPEN_QUESTIONS]]), Scenario B impact on yields may be ~30% less severe.

3. **Russian fertilizer arbitrage.** Turkey has maintained pragmatic ties with Russia. Russian urea (not under Western sanctions) likely backfills Gulf loss at ~$50–80/MT premium. This is a real mitigant. I am probably underweighting it.

4. **Grey-market Iranian fertilizer.** Given Turkey–Iran border trade, some Iranian nitrogen product may continue flowing informally. Not large, but non-zero.

5. **Counter-challenge:** All of the above is **already priced** into the current state. The question isn't whether Turkey can cope in Scenario A (it can) but whether Scenario B is survivable without a crisis. My conviction on Scenario B severity therefore remains.

6. **Investment-specific risk:** Turkish small-cap names (BAGFS, HEKTS) have **liquidity risk** that is not obvious in "normal" markets. If the TRY breaks 50, foreign holders exit and liquidity evaporates. Any position sizing must account for exit capacity.

7. **Time-window risk:** I treat "April–May fertilizer window" as the critical variable. But Turkish farmers have adapted to previous shocks by **reducing application rates, not skipping application entirely**. The damage function may be more linear than I've drawn.
