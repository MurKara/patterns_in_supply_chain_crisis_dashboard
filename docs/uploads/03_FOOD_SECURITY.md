# Food Security and Global Hunger

> **Zoom Level:** Region (Level 1)
> **Upstream in cascade:** [[02_FERTILIZER_AND_AGRICULTURE]] → THIS
> **Also connected to:** [[01_OIL_AND_ENERGY]] (transport costs), [[08_SHIPPING_AND_TRADE]] (delivery delays)

---

## Scale of the Threat

The UN World Food Programme projects an additional **45 million people** could be pushed into acute hunger by end of 2026 if the conflict persists. This would bring global food insecurity to levels not seen since the start of the Ukraine war.

---

## Transmission Mechanisms

The crisis hits food security through **three simultaneous channels:**

### Channel 1: Fertilizer shortage → Lower yields
- [[02_FERTILIZER_AND_AGRICULTURE]]: Urea +77%, DAP >$700/MT
- Farmers reduce application → yields drop
- Effect delayed by one growing season (3-6 months)

### Channel 2: Energy costs → Food production costs
- [[01_OIL_AND_ENERGY]]: Diesel for farming equipment, irrigation pumps
- Processing plant energy costs (milling, drying, cold chain)
- Immediate impact on production costs

### Channel 3: Shipping costs → Food delivery costs
- [[08_SHIPPING_AND_TRADE]]: Freight rates at all-time highs
- Insurance premiums multiplied 100x
- Grain shipments rerouted, delayed, or cancelled

---

## Food Price Projections

Under full Hormuz closure scenario:
- **Sri Lanka, Pakistan, India:** Food prices +10-15%
- **Global fertilizer prices:** +15-20% average in H1 2026
- **Apparel-food squeeze:** Low-income households face rising costs for both food AND clothing (see [[05_TEXTILES]])

---

## Compounding Factors

This crisis does not arrive in isolation:
- Post-COVID supply chain fragility still present
- Ukraine war already elevated baseline food prices
- Climate events (El Niño aftermath) affecting crop yields in some regions
- Several vulnerable countries already in debt distress
