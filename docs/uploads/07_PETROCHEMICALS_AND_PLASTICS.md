# Petrochemicals and Plastics

> **Zoom Level:** Region (Level 1)
> **Upstream in cascade:** [[01_OIL_AND_ENERGY]] → THIS
> **Downstream:** [[05_TEXTILES]], [[02_FERTILIZER_AND_AGRICULTURE]], [[04_SEMICONDUCTORS]] (neon), packaging, medical supplies

---

## The Hub Role

Petrochemicals sit at a **critical junction** in the cascade. Oil and gas are not just fuel — they are **feedstock** for thousands of products. The Middle East is a major petrochemical exporter because of cheap hydrocarbon inputs. Hormuz closure blocks both the fuel AND the feedstock.

---

## Key Blocked Materials

| Material | Middle East Share | Uses |
|----------|------------------|------|
| **LPG** (liquefied petroleum gas) | ~30% of global seaborne exports | Petrochemical feedstock, heating |
| **Naphtha** | ~24% of global seaborne trade | Feedstock for olefins, aromatics |
| **Polyethylene (PE)** | ~84% of ME PE capacity exports via Hormuz | Packaging, film, containers |
| **Methanol** | ~33% of global seaborne trade | Resins, coatings, plastics, formaldehyde |
| **Ethylene/Propylene** | Significant ME share | Building blocks for most plastics |

---

## Transmission to Downstream Sectors

```
Oil/gas feedstock blocked
    │
    ├─→ Naphtha shortage
    │       └─→ Olefins (ethylene, propylene) shortage
    │               ├─→ Polyethylene (PE) → packaging, film
    │               ├─→ Polypropylene (PP) → automotive, medical
    │               └─→ PVC → construction piping
    │
    ├─→ Natural gas shortage
    │       ├─→ Ammonia → [[02_FERTILIZER_AND_AGRICULTURE]]
    │       ├─→ Methanol → resins, coatings, [[05_TEXTILES]] inputs
    │       └─→ Neon (byproduct of air separation) → [[04_SEMICONDUCTORS]]
    │
    └─→ LPG shortage
            └─→ Steam cracker feedstock → all downstream plastics
```

## The Energy-vs-Feedstock Triage

East Asian countries face a brutal **prioritization dilemma** (noted by Atlantic Council):

> If Middle East LNG production outages force electricity rationing, governments must choose what to curtail. Options include petrochemical production, semiconductor fabrication, residential cooling, or other uses.

This means **sectors compete against each other** for the same scarce energy:
- [[04_SEMICONDUCTORS]] vs. [[07_PETROCHEMICALS_AND_PLASTICS]] vs. residential power
- Governments will prioritize: defense > residential > high-value manufacturing > commodity chemicals

---

## Geopolitical Implications

The Atlantic Council notes this crisis **benefits Russia and China:**
- Russia gains leverage as alternative hydrocarbon supplier
- China's domestic petrochemical capacity (less Hormuz-dependent) becomes a competitive advantage
- US consumers face higher plastics and packaging costs

---

## Medical Supply Risk

Often overlooked: many **medical supplies** depend on petrochemical-derived plastics:
- Syringes, IV bags, tubing, gloves, masks
- Pharmaceutical packaging
- Medical device housings
- This creates a potential **healthcare supply vulnerability** downstream
