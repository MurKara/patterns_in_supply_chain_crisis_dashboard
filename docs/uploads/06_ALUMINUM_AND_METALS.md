## Why Aluminum Is Hit

Aluminum smelting is one of the most **energy-intensive industrial processes** on Earth. The Middle East became a major aluminum hub precisely because of cheap energy from natural gas. The crisis hits aluminum through two channels simultaneously:

### Channel 1: Physical production disruption
- **Ras Laffan (Qatar)** and Gulf smelters directly in conflict zone
- **Al Taweelah smelter (EGA, UAE):** Precision strikes damaged power infrastructure
- **Alba smelter (Bahrain):** Also hit
- EGA reported "significant damage" → metal **frozen in potlines** (catastrophic; takes **>1 year to repair**)

### Channel 2: Energy costs
- Smelters that survived are paying drastically more for energy
- LNG-powered electricity generation costs have spiked
- Some smelters may curtail production voluntarily to avoid losses

## Production Loss

~9% of global primary aluminum production is sidelined or trapped behind the blockade. This is compounded by a **concurrent Guinea bauxite export restriction** — Guinea supplies ~25% of global bauxite (the ore for aluminum). A "perfect storm" scenario.

## The Potline Freeze Problem

When a smelter loses power, molten aluminum solidifies in the reduction cells ("potlines"). This is essentially **irreversible damage** — the solidified metal must be chipped out, and cells rebuilt. Recovery timeline: **12-18 months minimum**. This means even if the strait reopens tomorrow, some production capacity is lost for over a year.

---

## Turkey-Specific Note

Turkey is one of the **key customers** of Middle Eastern aluminum (see [[ENTITIES]]). Turkey's construction and automotive sectors depend on aluminum imports. Combined with Turkey's own energy import dependency, this creates a compounded vulnerability.
