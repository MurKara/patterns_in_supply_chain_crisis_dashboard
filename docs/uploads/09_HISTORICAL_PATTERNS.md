## Recurring Patterns

### Pattern 1: Geopolitical trigger → Supply shock → Price spike → Demand destruction
Every oil crisis follows this sequence. The difference is in **scale** and **transmission speed**. In 2026, global supply chains are far more interconnected than in the 1970s, so transmission is faster and broader.

### Pattern 2: Energy costs amplify through manufacturing chains
Higher energy costs don't just raise fuel prices — they cascade into every energy-intensive process. This was visible in 1973 (manufacturing recession) and is massively amplified in 2026 because:
- More industries are energy-intensive (semiconductors, aluminum)
- Supply chains are longer and more complex
- Just-in-time manufacturing has zero buffer

### Pattern 3: Fertilizer → Food → Social instability
The 1973 crisis contributed to food price inflation in developing countries. The 2022 Ukraine crisis showed the same pattern (wheat, fertilizer). In 2026, the **scale of fertilizer disruption (~30% of trade)** is unprecedented.

### Pattern 4: Insurance/shipping costs as hidden amplifier
In every crisis, the **financial infrastructure of trade** (insurance, letters of credit, freight rates) acts as an independent amplifier. Goods may be physically available but financially untransportable. See [[08_SHIPPING_AND_TRADE]].

### Pattern 5: Crisis reveals hidden dependencies
- 1973: Revealed Western dependence on OPEC oil
- 1979: Revealed vulnerability to single-country disruption
- 2022: Revealed European gas dependency on Russia; neon dependency on Ukraine
- **2026: Revealed semiconductor dependency on Gulf helium/neon; fertilizer chokepoint; aluminum energy nexus**

### Pattern 6: Demand shifts and substitution (delayed)
After every crisis, demand shifts occur — but with **multi-year lag**:
- Post-1973: Fuel efficiency standards, nuclear investment
- Post-1979: Diversification from OPEC, North Sea/Alaska production
- Post-2022: European LNG infrastructure, renewable acceleration
- Post-2026: `[OPEN QUESTION]` What structural shifts will this trigger?

## What's Different About 2026

1. **Scale:** 20M bbl/day vs. 4.5M in 1973 — the largest supply disruption ever
2. **Breadth:** Not just oil — LNG, fertilizer, helium, aluminum, neon, methanol all blocked simultaneously
3. **Interconnection:** Modern supply chains mean disruption propagates faster and wider
4. **Precedent awareness:** Markets and governments learned from 2022 (stockpiling, diversification) — but clearly not enough
5. **Military dimension:** Active combat zone (unlike embargoes, this involves mines and missile strikes)
6. **Physical infrastructure damage:** Smelters frozen, LNG facilities hit — recovery takes years, not weeks

## Lessons for the [[CRISIS_PATTERN_FRAMEWORK]]

From historical analysis, any future crisis checklist should ask:

1. **What is the chokepoint?** (Physical, financial, regulatory?)
2. **What percentage of global flow does it control?**
3. **What are the non-obvious dependencies?** (Helium for chips was invisible before 2026)
4. **What is the substitution timeline?** (Days? Months? Years?)
5. **What is the buffer/stockpile?** (Strategic reserves, warehouse inventory)
6. **Who is most exposed?** (Import-dependent, energy-intensive, thin margins)
7. **What are the financial amplifiers?** (Insurance, credit, currency)
8. **What planting/production seasons are affected?** (Timing matters enormously)
