## Why Textiles Are Hit

Polyester and its derivatives occupy **more than 50% of the global textile market**. Virgin polyester is the most direct "downstream carrier" of crude oil in apparel. The transmission follows a three-tier mechanism:

```
Crude oil supply disrupted (Hormuz)
    │
    ├─→ Tier 1: Upstream crude supply disruption
    │
    ├─→ Tier 2: Midstream feedstock spike
    │       ├─→ Olefins (ethylene, propylene)
    │       ├─→ Aromatics (paraxylene → PTA)
    │       └─→ MEG (monoethylene glycol)
    │
    └─→ Tier 3: Downstream fiber cost
            ├─→ PTA + MEG = Polyester chip raw materials (+15-25%)
            ├─→ Polyester staple fiber
            ├─→ Polyester filament yarn
            └─→ Nylon, acrylic, spandex (all petroleum-derived)
```

## Compounding Factors

### Energy costs for manufacturing
- South Asia (Bangladesh, Vietnam, India) — major garment producers — face **energy shortages**
- Natural gas constraints → electricity costs rising
- Air freight rates surged **+70%** (for fast fashion replenishment)

### Market shift to natural fibers
- As synthetic fiber prices climb, demand shifts toward **cotton**
- US cotton futures pushed to ~70 cents/lb
- But cotton has its own supply constraints (land, water, climate)
- This shift adds **upward price pressure across ALL fibers**

## Consumer Impact

- Low-income households face a **double squeeze**: rising food prices (see [[03_FOOD_SECURITY]]) AND rising clothing costs
- Fast fashion supply chains most exposed (thin margins, high synthetic content)
- Luxury/premium brands with natural fiber focus less directly exposed
