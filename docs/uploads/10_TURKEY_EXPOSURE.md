## Turkey's Structural Vulnerability — The Baseline

Before assessing sector impacts, understand Turkey's structural exposure:

| Indicator | Value | Implication |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| Energy import dependency | ~90% of total energy | Most energy is imported |
| Annual energy import bill | ~$65 billion/year | 3.5–4.5% of GDP |
| Oil price sensitivity | +$4.5–5B current account deficit per $10/bbl rise | Immediate balance-of-payments pressure |
| 2026 budget oil assumption | $65/bbl | Brent now at $104/bbl — **budget blown by ~60%** |
| TRY/USD (April 2026) | 44.5 TRY/USD (record low) | Import costs in TRY are compounded |
| Inflation (Dec 2025) | ~44% (declining trend from 75% peak) | Oil shock reverses disinflation |
| Iran gas share | ~14% of Turkey's nat-gas imports | Sanctioned supplier, partially lost |
| Russia gas share | ~37% of nat-gas imports | Alternative constrained by sanctions context |
| Azerbaijan gas share | ~21% of nat-gas imports | Southern Gas Corridor — key relief valve |

**Core dynamic:** Turkey is caught between two shocks simultaneously — a **supply shock** (energy access and cost) and a **currency/inflation shock** (TRY depreciation amplifying import prices in domestic terms). The Central Bank of Turkey (CBRT) has **paused its easing cycle**, eliminating the rate-cut tailwind Turkey was counting on in 2026.

## Sector Analysis

### 1. Energy & Refining — CRITICAL IMPACT

**Probability of severe impact: 95%**

#### The Chokepoint Hit

Turkey relies on seaborne crude imports from the Middle East. With Hormuz closed:
- Gulf crude (Saudi, UAE, Kuwait) — **blocked**
- Iranian gas (14% of supply) — **already sanctioned/blocked**
- Spot LNG from Qatar — **blocked** (QatarEnergy force majeure)

#### The Partial Mitigant

The **Kirkuk–Ceyhan Oil Pipeline** (Iraq → Turkey's Mediterranean coast at Ceyhan) is **the single most important mitigant** for Turkey:
- Restarted March 17, 2026 at **170,000 bpd** (ramping to 250,000 bpd)
- Theoretical capacity: **1.5 million bpd** (massively underutilised)
- Iraq accounts for ~20% of Turkey's total oil imports and **bypasses Hormuz entirely**
- Turkey is actively negotiating **Basra extension** — connecting to southern Iraq fields
- **Development Road** plan: full Gulf-to-Turkey-to-Europe transport corridor under discussion

**But:** Iraq's capacity to export via Ceyhan is limited by political disputes (Kurdistan regional government, Iraqi central government). Ramping to full 1.5M bpd capacity would take 12–18+ months of infrastructure investment.

#### Key Company: TÜPRAŞ (TUPRS.IS)

Turkey's **sole oil refiner** and one of the largest companies in Turkey (Koç Holding subsidiary).

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Role | Refines ~28M tonnes/year crude; sole domestic refiner |
| Exposure | Crude supply disruption; feedstock cost surge |
| Mitigant | Kirkuk-Ceyhan crude; potential to increase Russian crude via Black Sea |
| Risk | Margin squeeze if crude prices rise faster than product prices (regulated) |
| Probability of significant earnings hit | **90%** |
| Short-term upside? | If margins are passed through, revenue rises but volumes fall |

> **TÜPRAŞ is the single most exposed large-cap Turkish company to this crisis.** It sits at the intersection of oil supply, refining margins, and domestic fuel prices.

### 2. Textiles & Apparel — HIGH IMPACT

**Probability of significant impact: 80%**

Turkey is the **4th largest textile exporter globally** (~$30+ billion in annual exports). The sector is deeply exposed to Hormuz because:

1. **Polyester feedstock:** Turkey imports ~$700–900M/year of **monoethylene glycol (MEG)** — 35–40% from Gulf producers. MEG is stranded behind the blockade.
2. **Energy costs:** Synthetic fibre production is energy-intensive; electricity/gas costs have surged.
3. **Petrochemical inputs:** Nylon, PET, and other synthetics sourced from Gulf petrochemicals — see [[07_PETROCHEMICALS_AND_PLASTICS]].

**Price impact:** Polyester feedstocks have surged **25–40%** since crisis onset. Globally, textile production costs are up **10–15%**.

#### Key Companies

| Company | Ticker | Exposure | Detail |
|---------|--------|----------|--------|
| **Korteks** | private | HIGH | Turkey's largest polyester yarn producer; directly exposed to MEG prices |
| **Isko Denim** | private | HIGH | Premium denim; synthetic blend feedstocks |
| **Sanko Tekstil** | private | HIGH | Major synthetic yarn/fabric; Gulf feedstock dependent |
| **Arçelik** | ARCLK.IS | MEDIUM | Home appliances with plastics/synthetics content; energy cost pass-through |
| **Sabancı / Aksa** | AKSA.IS | HIGH | Acrylic fibre producer; petrochemical feedstock dependent |

**Competitive angle:** Chinese textiles face the same feedstock shock — Turkey may actually gain relative market share **if** it can source MEG from alternative suppliers (US, European). [VERIFY — US MEG export capacity in 2026]

### 3. Agriculture & Fertilizer — HIGH IMPACT (Compounded)

> **📂 Full deep dive with scenarios, company signals and watchlist: [[11_TURKEY_FERTILIZER_DEEP_DIVE]]**

**Probability of food inflation surge: 85%**

Turkey is ranked **2nd most vulnerable globally** (after Brazil) to Hormuz-linked fertilizer disruption. The timing is catastrophic:

- **2025 drought:** Agricultural output was already hit by record-low rainfall; yields contracted.
- **Hormuz closure:** ~30% of global fertilizer trade (urea, nitrogen, phosphate) transits Hormuz.
- **Fertilizer prices:** Up **40%** since blockade began.

#### The Double Hit

```
2025 Drought (lower yields)
    +
2026 Fertilizer Shortage (+40% prices, supply cuts)
    =
2026–2027 Harvest Crisis → Domestic Food Inflation
```

**Food inflation projections for Turkey (economists' scenarios):**

| Scenario | Duration | Additional Food Inflation |
|----------|----------|--------------------------|
| Short crisis | 3 months | +0.5 to +1.5 pp |
| Medium crisis | 6 months | +1.5 to +4.0 pp |
| Prolonged crisis | 9+ months | +4.0 to +8.0 pp (estimated) |

Given that Turkey's overall inflation is already ~44%, a 4–8pp food inflation surge would be deeply destabilising for social stability and CBRT policy.

#### Key Companies

| Company | Ticker | Exposure | Detail |
|---------|--------|----------|--------|
| **Gübre Fabrikaları (Gübretaş)** | GUBRF.IS | CRITICAL | Turkey's main fertilizer importer/distributor; sourcing disrupted |
| **Toros Agri** | private (Sabancı group) | HIGH | Fertilizer distribution; Gulf sourcing dependent |
| **BİM / Migros / Şok** | BIMAS.IS / MGROS.IS | MEDIUM | Grocery retailers; input cost pass-through → margin squeeze or price hikes |

### 4. Aluminum & Metals — HIGH IMPACT

**Probability of significant impact: 85%**

Turkey is one of the top customers for Gulf-produced primary aluminum (alongside USA and Japan — the three account for 35% of Gulf aluminum exports). With UAE's EGA Al Taweelah and Bahrain's Alba smelters damaged, primary aluminum supply is constrained. See [[06_ALUMINUM_AND_METALS]].

**LME Aluminum:** Near a 4-year high (approaching $3,500/tonne as of April 2026). Hot-rolled coil (HRC) steel broke $1,000/tonne threshold.

#### Key Companies

| Company | Ticker | Exposure | Detail |
|---------|--------|----------|--------|
| **Assan Aluminium** | private (Sabancı) | HIGH | Turkey's largest flat-rolled aluminum manufacturer; feedstock (primary Al) price surge |
| **Erdemir / Ereğli** | EREGL.IS | MEDIUM–HIGH | Steel production; energy costs rising; scrap steel input partly domestic, but electricity cost is key |
| **Borçelik** | private (Koç) | MEDIUM | Flat steel; similar energy exposure to Erdemir |
| **İskenderun Demir Çelik (İSDEMİR)** | ISDMR.IS | HIGH | Integrated steel; Turkey's largest crude steel; energy-intensive |

**Erdemir upside:** Erdemir is also a **steel supplier to Turkey's military programs** (MUGEM aircraft carrier). Defense procurement may partially offset any slowdown in export demand.

### 5. Aviation — HIGH IMPACT (With Partial Upside)

**Probability of revenue impact: 90%**

Aviation is one of the most immediately affected sectors due to **jet fuel prices rising ~106%** since the war began (Feb 28). Fuel = 50%+ of airline operating costs.

#### Routes Suspended by Turkish Airlines (THYAO.IS)

Flights to Iraq, Syria, Lebanon, Jordan, Doha, Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Kuwait, Bahrain, Dammam, and Iran were suspended or reduced.

#### The Upside: Turkey as Diversion Hub

- **76+ international flights** diverted to Turkish airports since February 28
- Turkey's geographic position (bridge between Europe and Middle East) becomes an active routing advantage
- Turkish Airlines added capacity on alternative routes (Oman, indirect paths)
- Istanbul airports benefit from increased transfer traffic

#### Key Companies

| Company | Ticker | Net Impact | Detail |
|---------|--------|------------|--------|
| **Turkish Airlines (THY)** | THYAO.IS | NEGATIVE NET | Jet fuel +106%; ME routes suspended; diversion hub partially offsets |
| **Pegasus Airlines** | PGSUS.IS | NEGATIVE | Higher operating costs; fewer ME destinations |
| **TAV Airports** | TAVHL.IS | MIXED | Istanbul/Ankara: diversion traffic positive; ME airports (Tbilisi, Almaty exposure) — limited |
| **Turkish Technic** | private (THY subsidiary) | MEDIUM | Maintenance demand; fleet grounding offset by new routes |

**Tourism:** Eastern Turkey (Erzurum, Van, Doğubayazıt) hard hit by loss of Iranian tourist inflows. Western coast (Antalya, İzmir, Bodrum) relatively insulated but higher airfare costs reduce European demand.

### 6. Shipping & Logistics — HIGH IMPACT

**Probability of disruption: 95%**

- **14 Turkish-owned ships** stranded near Hormuz as of April 1, 2026
- War risk insurance premiums surged from 0.25% to **3% of vessel value**
- Turkey's container shipping and tanker operators face rerouting costs
- Spot charter rates for LNG carriers up **~600%**

**The Special Passage:** Iran allowed one Turkish-owned ship through Hormuz on March 13, signaling Turkey's somewhat neutral diplomatic status. This is geopolitically significant — Turkey is NOT in the coalition attacking Iran.

## Turkey's Strategic Opportunity: The Energy Corridor Play

**Probability of Turkey benefiting as corridor: 60% (12–18 month horizon); 80% (3–5 year horizon)**

This is the single most important **upside** for Turkey from the crisis:

### Kirkuk–Ceyhan Pipeline (Active)
- Currently flowing 170k–250k bpd; capacity 1.5M bpd
- **Proposed Basra Extension:** Would connect southern Iraqi fields; transport 300k–500k bpd initially
- Turkey has offered to host the project; Iraq and Gulf states interested
- **Contract renewal in July 2026** — critical negotiating moment

### Qatar LNG via Turkey
- Turkish Energy Minister Bayraktar proposed LNG pipeline from Qatar through Iran/Iraq land route to Turkey and onward to Europe
- Currently a proposal ("pipe dream" as one analyst noted — [VERIFY feasibility timeline])
- Europe desperate for non-Russian, non-Gulf alternatives → Turkey as transit hub value rises

### Southern Gas Corridor (Active and Strengthened)
- Azerbaijan → Georgia → Turkey → Southern Europe
- Already the **only major overland route** for non-Russian, non-Gulf gas into the EU
- TANAP + TAP pipelines operating at capacity; expansion discussions accelerated
- Directly benefits Turkish gas transit revenues

### Development Road (Iraq → Turkey)
- Iraq's $17B infrastructure project: road + rail + pipeline from Faw port (Persian Gulf) to Turkey
- Hormuz crisis is the **strongest possible argument** for this project's urgency
- If implemented, Turkey becomes the primary land corridor bypassing all maritime chokepoints
